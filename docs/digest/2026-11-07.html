Abstract

We consider distributed state estimation of a linear plant under stabilizing feedback over wireless multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) fading channels, where channel-estimation noise corrupts the channel state information (CSI) used for information fusion. We propose a distributed estimator that combines local prediction, communication over wireless MIMO channels, and a posterior update based on local information and communicated data. Each sensor uses noisy pilots to estimate its aggregated wireless MIMO fading channel, and a pilot-noise bias correction recovers the required neighboring-data differences in conditional mean. We first analyze the mean estimation error and show that a Chebyshev design of the communication weights achieves a prescribed decay factor $d\in(0,1)$ satisfying the sensing conditions, with finitely many communication updates per plant sample at every finite signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) gain. We then characterize mean-square stability and the effects of SNR gain and receive-array size on the state estimation mean-square error (MSE), including its transient and steady-state value. In the ideal-communication limit, we show that the square root of the absolute MSE deviation from its steady-state value decays as $o(d^k)$. Numerical simulations verify the theoretical results and show substantial performance gain compared to various state-of-the-art baselines.

Abstract

Multisensory Physical AI systems operating in isolated conditions must make timely and reliable decisions within strict energy and hardware budgets. In this context, the present analytical study investigates the feasibility of reliable multimodal sensor fusion using a neuromorphic approach based on memristor-based Integrate-and-Fire neuron circuits driven by aggressively compressed representations of sensory information. Rather than relying on fixed processing and learning parameters, the proposed framework introduces scenario-dependent adaptability into individual stages of the neuromorphic decision-making pipeline, including sensor-data compression, nonlinear spike encoding, reliability assessment, and memristive learning-forgetting dynamics. By coupling multiple sensor inputs through adaptive memristive synapses, the framework investigates how reliable and correlated observations can strengthen learned associations, while weak or obsolete relationships gradually decay through nonlinear forgetting. The resulting neuronal responses are evaluated across ten representative driving scenarios derived from the NuScenes dataset by comparing their encoded output patterns with predefined synthetic ground-truth codes representing the expected actuator responses. The simulations demonstrate that scenario-adaptive activity-vector sizing achieves a 61.5% reduction in retained sensory information, while improving mean bit-wise agreement from approximately 55% to 61.2% compared with a fixed maximum-resolution configuration. These findings highlight the potential of introducing scenario-aware adaptability directly into the building blocks of a neuromorphic system, providing a foundation for future hardware implementations that could significantly transform the capabilities of Physical AI systems operating under stringent resource and environmental constraints.

Abstract

This paper presents a decentralized guiding vector field framework for rigid formation path following in multi- UAV systems based on the superposition of simple rule-driven vector fields. Each control objective is encoded as a dedicated vector-field component: a normalized tangential term to ensure progression along a prescribed geometric path at a desired speed, a convergence term driving the formation toward the path, a local coordination term that preserves the desired formation shape through a pseudo-path parameter, and a local separation term for inter-agent collision avoidance. The coordination mechanism requires the exchange of only a single scalar per agent, resulting in low communication bandwidth. Partial theoretical results establish convergence of the swarm to the desired path and formation in the absence of the sepa- ration term, while collision avoidance is formally guaranteed. The approach is experimentally validated using a swarm of five quadrotors executing two 3D choreographies. The first experiment involves tracking a helical path while maintaining a rigid V-shaped formation. The second demonstrates a spiral- like motion obtained by a rotating circular formation tracking a vertical line. The experimental results support the effectiveness and practical applicability of the proposed framework.

Abstract

This paper studies dual path following for a quadrotor carrying a three-degree-of-freedom arm. The center of mass of the combined system must follow a vehicle path and the end effector must follow a second path, while the arm respects its joint limits and avoids its singular postures. The vehicle and the arm are mechanically coupled, so one controller must handle both paths. With the center of mass and the end-effector path coordinates as outputs, and a dynamic extension of the thrust, the system admits a normal form without internal dynamics. We characterize the states where this normal form fails. We formulate the transverse feedback linearizing controller as a quadratic program that relaxes only the speed of the end effector along its path and imposes safety and actuator limits as inequality constraints. The program reduces to a scalar problem with a closed-form solution. If it remains strictly feasible for all time, the feedback is locally Lipschitz, both paths are invariant and attractive, and every constraint holds. In the physics engine Drake, the controller resolves a conflict between the two speed references by slowing the end effector while both paths remain invariant. Abstract

Production wheel-speed sensors deliver only a few pulses per revolution, so they lose resolution below 1 km/h, the regime in which automated parking and traffic-jam assistance operate. The motor speed from the electric machine's resolver stays finely resolved down to standstill but reaches the wheels only through a compliant drivetrain. We therefore estimate the longitudinal velocity with an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) on a double-track model adapted to low velocities and incorporate the motor speed along two routes, one purely physical and one hybrid. The first augments the model with an explicit torsional spring-damper drivetrain, making the motor speed a state of the estimator. Abstract

Digital Twins are evolving from passive virtual representations into closed-loop systems that continuously synchronize with physical assets and drive real-time decisions. Yet predictive modeling, machine learning (ML), uncertainty quantification (UQ), and decision-making have been studied as separate capabilities, leaving limited understanding of their integration and maintenance during online operation. This narrative review, scoped by the functional requirements of real-time decision-making rather than by Digital Twin terminology alone, examines how such decisions are generated and sustained in engineering Digital Twins. We first examine foundational decision-making architectures and representative engineering applications, followed by methods that address their limitations across the decision loop. We identify optimization-based, policy-based, and knowledge-based approaches as the major pathways from virtual representation to action. Across them, ML plays three roles in decision-making: enabling efficient surrogate modeling, transforming heterogeneous physical feedback into decision-relevant information, and accelerating or directly generating decisions. UQ supports uncertainty-aware decisions and model adaptation, while continual validation, drift detection, and adaptation help maintain reliable operation as the physical system evolves. Together, these findings underscore the need for a shift from a model-centric to a system-centric perspective on Digital Twins, where its performance depends on the interactions with sensing, state estimation, computation, decision-making, and adaptation rather than on their standalone performance. Therefore, future research should shift from isolated algorithm development toward system-level co-design, scalable decision-making, and approaches to design, and data-efficient continual adaptation and validation for AI-enabled autonomous systems.
