Abstract

Accurate end-effector trajectory tracking on quadruped manipulators is challenging because uncertainties in actuators, foot-ground contact, and geometric errors are propagated through the leg-arm kinematic chain. Detecting such errors requires sensing beyond joint encoders that can capture the effects of these uncertainties on the robot's motion, since joint encoders only provide measurements at the actuated joints and cannot fully observe mechanical errors such as backlash, dimensional inaccuracies, or structural deformation. This work addresses these challenges using a reinforcement learning framework with end-effector (EE) inertial feedback, targeting local high-accuracy manipulation while maintaining a nominal body pose and support configuration without intentional body motion or foot repositioning. The learned policy uses EE inertial measurements to compensate for tracking errors induced by uncertainties throughout the leg-arm system that cannot be fully inferred from proprioception alone. Simulation and real-world experiments on continuous EE trajectory tracking show overall improvements relative to nominal and proprioception-only baselines. In real-world experiments, the proposed policy reduces the MoCap-based RMSE by 17.3% and 28.0% relative to the nominal baseline under the two in-distribution (ID) command conditions, respectively, and by an additional 12.6% and 15.9% relative to the proprioception-only baseline. Furthermore, the trajectory-wise RMSE between FK-reconstructed and motion-capture-measured end-effector positions averaged 12.61+-2.15mm (mean+-standard deviation) across 24 controller-trajectory combinations, revealing a substantial discrepancy between nominal kinematic reconstruction and measured end-effector motion.

Abstract

Reliable proprioceptive state estimation for legged robots fuses inertial sensing with kinematic sensing from leg encoders. The latter assumes a foot is stationary during the stance phase; however, physical foot touchdown does not necessarily provide a stationary reference for odometry, particularly during impact and load transfer. Repeatedly applying kinematic corrections throughout the same contact phase can introduce redundant or unreliable information. We present SG-ODOM, a proprioceptive odometry framework that uses stability-gated contact selection to schedule kinematic updates. Following physical touchdown, a stability gate accepts the contact for state estimation only after the foot reaches a sufficiently stable support condition. The resulting validated touchdown event triggers an estimator correction, while additional timeout updates bound the maximum duration of uninterrupted IMU propagation. The framework is evaluated on multiple legged platforms, both quadrupedal and humanoid robots, against representative state-of-the-art proprioceptive estimators. Across the evaluated sequences, SG-ODOM demonstrates competitive or superior estimation accuracy, reducing Relative Pose Error (RPE) by up to 25% compared to the baseline estimator on outdoor sequences from the GrandTour dataset. Abstract

Data-driven predictive control (DDPC) predicts system responses from recorded trajectories, but nonlinear dynamics can limit the accuracy of fixed linear predictors. This paper studies geometric robust DDPC for trajectory tracking of a fully actuated manipulator using model-based feedback linearisation. A mechanical model maps virtual acceleration commands into joint torques, while recorded data capture residual dynamics arising from imperfect model parameters. The controller minimises worst-case prediction error over an open Grassmannian ball centred at the fitted trajectory subspace. Simulations on a planar 2R manipulator under gravity compare the proposed method with double-integrator model predictive control (MPC) and direct regularised data-enabled predictive control (DeePC). Results show improved tracking over double-integrator MPC despite increased torque effort, while performance relative to DeePC depends on the reference trajectory.

Abstract

We develop a methodology for set-based state estimation from noisy input-output trajectories, without a predefined model structure for the unknown state dynamics or access to training state measurements. We derive a bound on the state set diameter that depends on the sensor map and leverage it as a surrogate to formulate a tractable linear program balancing state set tightness against output trajectory coverage. Numerical simulations corroborate our developed theoretical guarantees.
