Abstract

Distribution System State Estimation (DSSE) is a crucial tool for real-time monitoring, operation, and control of the new-age distribution systems (DSs) due to intensified voltage fluctuations and operational uncertainties introduced by volatile power injection at the nodes. Existing GNN-based approaches generally exhibit limited generalization across networks with different sizes and topologies, making zero-shot cross-grid transfer to unseen DSs a significant challenge. To overcome the aforementioned challenges, this article proposes an Inductive Physics-Informed Graph Neural Operator (IP-GNO) for scalable and transferable DSSE. The proposed framework frames complex bus voltage estimation as an operator-learning problem inspired by the Neumann series expansion of the inverse bus admittance matrix. An admittance-parameterized kernel, incorporating a self-admittance gate in the zeroth-order term and a persistent source-injection skip connection across network layers, captures the underlying electrical dependencies. Additionally, a Physics-Embedded Tree Encoding (PETE) scheme introduces five grid-size-invariant positional features derived from path impedance and a DC-linearized phase-angle prior. A sine-cosine output formulation is further employed to address phase-angle periodicity and ensure stable training. The proposed IP-GNO module is evaluated on two small (IEEE 33- and 69-bus), two medium (IEEE 123- and 136-bus), and one large (2522-bus) sized DSs. Particularly, its zero-shot cross-grid generalization capability is investigated by training the model exclusively on the IEEE 33-bus system and directly evaluating it on four unseen distribution networks of different sizes without retraining or fine-tuning. A detailed comparative analysis with the existing state-of-the-art DSSE tools proves the superiority of IP-GNO, enabling scalable DSSE across heterogeneous DSs.

Abstract

High positioning precision is a critical requirement in many Global Positioning System (GPS) applications, yet achieving it with affordable smartphone sensors remains an open challenge. Existing approaches treat each GPS observation as an absolute point, discarding the uncertainty inherent in the measurement conditions and leaving the cooperative potential of multiple phones observing the same location untapped. In this work, we propose GeoFusion, a three-stage uncertain machine learning framework for cooperative sensor fusion. In the first stage, each GPS observation is modeled as an uncertain object using one of two proposed models: the volcano function, grounded in satellite geometry, and the empirically derived mountain function, built from the statistical structure of real smartphone data. In the second stage, uncertain objects are clustered by location event using partitional and graph-based algorithms. In the third stage, the cluster output is refined using filter-based and learning-based estimators. Experiments on the Google Smartphone Decimeter Challenge dataset show that uncertainty modeling is a primary driver of precision gains: the mountain function significantly outperforms the geometry-only volcano function, and uncertainty-aware UK-medoids significantly outperforms its certain counterpart, k-medoids. A post-clustering deep neural network further refines the cluster estimates, and the recommended configuration achieves a mean absolute error of 2.90 +/- 0.15 meters on held-out test drives, comparable to and on average below the best reported single-phone precision of approximately 3 meters, demonstrating the potential of cooperative uncertainty-aware fusion for affordable GPS sensors.

Abstract

Recovering articulated robot state from video supports metric spatial sensing for manipulation, scene reconstruction, and action planning. Yet the problem is ill-posed: different intrinsics, camera-to-robot poses, and joint angles can explain similar observations. Prior task-specific regressors typically assume known intrinsics, fit one robot through laborious data collection and robot-specific training, and still transfer poorly across scenes. Abstract

Accurate trajectory tracking alone does not guarantee safe quadrotor flight when the reference passes near obstacles or the available control authority is limited. This paper presents a safety-filtered trajectory-tracking framework for a quadrotor operating at a fixed altitude in obstacle-cluttered environments. A nominal tracking controller is combined with high-order control barrier constraints and an acceleration-constrained quadratic program. The safety filter preserves the nominal acceleration whenever it is admissible and modifies it only when the prescribed obstacle clearance is threatened. The analysis establishes nominal exponential tracking, forward invariance of the safe region under compatible initial conditions and continued quadratic-program feasibility, and bounded tracking error during safety intervention. Numerical experiments using an idealized planar translational model with single and multiple stationary circular obstacles show minimum clearances of $0.000$--$0.001$~m under the safety filter, compared with $-1.594/-1.421$~m under nominal control. Tracking RMSE increases from $0.618$ to $0.950$~m across reference-speed scales $0.6$--$1.6$, illustrating the effect of reference speed on tracking accuracy under safety constraints. Additional tests examine how acceleration limits affect control effort and feasibility. These findings inform safety-filter design for UAV trajectory tracking under limited control authority.

Conformal prediction quantifies uncertainty with distribution-free guarantees, but existing methods do not address the challenging combination of dependence among robots, distributed calibration, and the distribution shift induced when calibrated uncertainty bounds modify the closed-loop system. We introduce Multi-Agent Interactive Conformal Prediction (MA-ICP), a distributed framework for computing robustness margins in heterogeneous systems to satisfy mission requirements with high probability. MA-ICP aggregates robot-level nonconformity scores, which measure errors in modeled or estimated quantities, into one mission-level score per robot class, allowing dependence among robots within each mission. Mission-level scores and quantiles are computed using neighbor-to-neighbor communication in a finite number of communication rounds. Updating the robustness margins changes robot trajectories and distributed algorithm outputs, so we use class-dependent score sensitivities to upper bound the shift in mission-level scores. We then establish calibration-conditional coverage guarantees per robot class for the updated margins. We demonstrate MA-ICP on cooperative payload control, formation control with obstacle avoidance, and distributed target tracking missions.
