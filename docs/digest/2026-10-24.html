有些无人机想往前飞时不用整个机身歪过去，而是把螺旋桨转个角度，但这样飞行更难稳，风一吹容易停不住、偏出去。作者在电脑里搭了一套飞行控制方案：飞行阶段变了就换用相应的定位办法，再让一个很小的学习补丁在原有控制上做有限修正，专门抵消风造成的偏差。好处是电脑模拟里能更准地知道自己在哪，被风吹着停住时偏离目标大约少一半，风比训练时更大也仍有明显改善。

Abstract

Tilt-rotor quadcopters redirect thrust for translation without banking, but complicate modeling, state estimation, and disturbance rejection. This paper presents a simulation-based flight stack for a \SI{2.00}{\kilo\gram} tilt-rotor quadcopter. The system uses a six-degree-of-freedom nonlinear model with first-order motor dynamics and two-axis rotor tilt, parameterized using a 3D CAD model. A cascaded PD controller commands forward and lateral tilt angles. A 14-state EKF fuses sensor data across hover, early-transition, and transition modes using a hysteretic (Schmitt-trigger) classifier to prevent covariance discontinuities. Because the proportional position loop leaves a steady-state offset under constant wind, a small bounded neural residual is added on top of the unmodified PD controller. It is trained by behavioral cloning from a teacher with privileged access to the true wind, while the deployed network never observes wind. In simulation, the EKF achieves a post-settling position root mean square error (RMSE) of \SI{0.226}{\metre} (horizontal) and \SI{0.03}{\metre} (vertical) and an attitude RMSE below \SI{0.8}{\degree}, and it estimates a wind velocity of $(\num{2.0}, \num{-1.2})\,\unit{\metre\per\second}$ to within \SI{0.15}{\metre\per\second} per axis. The residual reduces steady-state station-keeping error under wind by approximately \SI{49}{\percent}, from \SI{0.117}{\metre} to \SI{0.060}{\metre} at \SI{1.5}{\metre\per\second} and from \SI{0.319}{\metre} to \SI{0.162}{\metre} at an out-of-distribution \SI{3.9}{\metre\per\second}. The framework provides a basis for hardware-in-the-loop and flight-test studies.

Abstract

Industries employ robots for repetitive tasks due to their high precision and performance, typically achieved through feedforward control actions based on an accurate robot description. However, an exact dynamic model can be difficult to obtain, especially for robots with elastic elements or when the payload changes. Learning-based feedforward approaches are promising for trajectory tracking under model uncertainties, but typically face generalization issues when task parameters vary. This paper proposes a novel method to generalize the feedforward control action with respect to payload parameters, independently of the underlying controller. Firstly, the minimal number of example objects needed for payload generalization is derived. Then, an algorithm is proposed to execute the same trajectory with a new payload, given only its parameters, without knowledge of the robot's dynamic model and even in the presence of elastic joints. The effectiveness of the proposed algorithm is validated in simulations and experiments on tracking tasks with different robots and controllers. Two sets of experiments are performed: on a standard Franka Emika Panda robot, and on a Franka Emika Panda robot equipped with an elastic wrist.

Abstract

Traditional robotic navigation systems separate mapping, planning, and low-level control through cascaded modules, introducing computational overhead and accumulated latency. End-to-end reinforcement learning offers a compact unified policy that maps onboard observations directly to control actions. However, on inherently unstable platforms such as two-wheeled inverted-pendulum robots, navigation progress and obstacle avoidance can conflict with attitude stabilization in a strongly state-dependent manner. We propose a lightweight state-dependent objective coordination method for integrated navigation and control. A pitch-conditioned scalarization coefficient $β(s)\in[0,1]$ adjusts the relative contributions of balance stabilization and navigation objectives during training, without extra control hierarchies or auxiliary attitude-stabilization controllers, such as proportional-integral-derivative (PID) or linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) controllers. To support zero-shot deployment, simulation training models execution delay, action hold, asynchronous and noisy observations, and dynamics uncertainty. Gradient analysis reveals persistent directional interference and state-dependent variation in the objectives' relative scales. Comparisons with alternative reward-weighting, constraint, training, and control-structure strategies show that PPO+$β(s)$, where PPO denotes proximal policy optimization, achieves 98% and 95% success rates with small attitude excursions on the fixed obstacle map and the randomly generated high-obstacle-density map, respectively. Zero-shot hardware experiments further demonstrate bounded attitude behavior and consistent obstacle avoidance using only onboard observations. Training and evaluation code will be made publicly available upon acceptance.

Abstract

End-to-end control policies trained via Reinforcement Learning (RL) have emerged as an alternative to conventional surgical robotic control approaches, with recent studies exploring state-based policies trained on privileged state observations. However, such approaches rely on precise physical states that are difficult to obtain reliably in surgical environments. Vision-based RL provides an alternative by learning manipulation policies directly from RGB observations, avoiding explicit state estimation and manually engineered perception pipelines while enabling policies to acquire task-relevant visual representations. Nevertheless, obtaining vision-based policies via pure RL methods for surgical manipulation remains challenging. Thus, imitation learning (IL) becomes another solution, yet it requires a large number of expert demonstrations, which are difficult to obtain in minimally invasive surgery. In this work, we propose a framework for state-to-vision policy distillation in laparoscopic robotic manipulation. Specifically, a state-based policy with privileged observations is employed as the teacher, and a vision-based policy learns from RGB observations and robot proprioceptive information, which requires less expert demonstrations than IL and introduces superior performance than RL methods. We conduct extensive experiments on various surgical manipulation tasks in the simulated environment. Experimental results show that, in the context of laparoscopic robotic manipulation, training vision-based policies with general-purpose RL methods is challenging, whereas our proposed state-to-vision policy distillation leads to more consistent and stable performance across tasks.

Abstract

This paper presents TRACE, a traversability-aware reactive navigation framework designed for the autonomous operation of ground robots in unstructured forest environments. Unlike traditional approaches, TRACE leverages VLM-based confidence maps from positive and negative natural-language prompt sets and combines them through evidential fusion to produce a dense traversability map that prioritizes the terrain based on signs of previously traversed paths. Traversability-annotated points are then extracted and used to derive the left and right boundaries of the traversable path via percentile binning followed by spline or regularized polynomial fitting. These boundaries are then incorporated as soft barrier constraints within an NMPC formulation, enabling safe and adaptive path following in complex natural environments. Rigorous experimental trials carried out in real-life forest environments demonstrate TRACE ability for smooth navigation in cluttered, deformable, and visually ambiguous terrains.

机器人叠衣服时，常常只能看到衣服的一部分，而且衣服很软、一碰就变形，所以很难判断它现在是什么形状、下一步会变成什么样。这篇论文让机器人先根据看到的一小部分，在“脑子里”补全衣服的整体形状和软硬特点，并预判自己怎么动会让衣服变成什么样，然后再安排动作。这样做的实际好处是，机器人面对没见过的衣服、不同的机器人和不同叠法时也能上手，不用重新训练，叠衣服更稳、更准。

AI 精读要点 · DeepSeek

创新点: 提出软物体世界模型，利用大规模仿真交互训练 transformer，并通过部分点云历史的上下文学习推断潜在形状与材质线索，实现跨机器人本体、材料、衣物类型和协作模式无需权重更新的泛化。

方法: 在仿真软物体交互数据上训练基于 transformer 的生成式全状态估计与动作条件动力学预测模型，采用缓解仿真到现实差距的措施，并将其与目标条件动作规划器结合进行双臂衣物折叠。

结论: 在仿真和真实世界双臂衣物折叠任务中，该方法在重建保真度、预测准确性和任务成功率上均取得提升。

Abstract

Robust manipulation of soft objects, such as garments, is a critical skill for service and household robots to adopt, but remains challenging due to its high-dimensional state space, and material-dependent deformations. Humans address this challenge by using mental world models that "imagine" the shape and material properties from partial observations, which enables them to predict how actions affect future states. As such, embedding such world models in robots allows for better generalization in manipulating a wide variety of garments in the real world. However, learned world models typically require large amounts of real world data that is strenuous to collect, and a mechanism to infer latent properties that govern dynamics. We present a Soft-Object World Model that performs generative full-state estimation and action-conditioned dynamics prediction using a transformer backbone trained on large-scale simulated soft object interactions, with measures to mitigate the sim-to-real gap. By learning to infer latent shape and material cues through in-context learning over a variable-length partial point-cloud history, the model generalizes across robot embodiments, material parameters, garment types and shapes, and manipulation coordination modes without weight updates. The world model is then integrated with a Goal-Conditioned action planner for motion synthesis. We validate the approach in simulation and real world for bimanual garment folding, reporting improvements in reconstruction fidelity, prediction accuracy, and task success.
