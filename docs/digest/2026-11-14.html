Abstract

In patrol missions involving multiple unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), a designated command USV can support fleet management by sequentially rendezvousing with each patrolling USV. These operations require motion-feasible route planning and priority-aware rendezvous sequencing, with the objective of minimizing mission completion time and total tardiness relative to priority-dependent due dates. We propose a framework combining grid-based path planning, surrogate travel-time prediction, and graph-based reinforcement learning. The path planner accounts for ocean currents and approximates turn-rate constraints through multi-stage maneuvers, with path feasibility empirically validated through trajectory-tracking simulations. For rendezvous sequencing, a Feasible Transition Graph (FTG) represents candidate rendezvous events and temporally feasible transitions, enabling a graph neural network to exploit spatiotemporal rendezvous relationships for policy learning. A surrogate predicts travel times from compact current and motion features to avoid repeated path searches during FTG construction and policy training. The policy achieves these improvements while requiring approximately 1/35 of the latter's computation time. It maintains its advantage over MLP-RL under unseen priority-tier settings, with larger gains as fleet size increases.

Abstract

Generative models can support both state estimation and control in robotics. For control, a common approach is to use the model as a policy that directly samples actions conditioned on observation histories. However, models that jointly represent states, actions, and observations can also predict how the robot state and its environment evolve. We investigate whether these predictions can improve state estimation and control through a change in inference procedure, without modifying the pretrained model. To this end, we use particle filtering to maintain competing hypotheses about state histories. A single pretrained diffusion model generates these hypotheses along with their predicted observations and associated action proposals. As new measurements arrive, particles are reweighted according to how well their predicted observations match the measurements. While particle filtering conventionally uses these weights only for state estimation, we also use them to guide action selection, favoring proposals associated with hypotheses that better explain the observations. Simulation experiments in partially observed navigation and vision-based manipulation demonstrate improvements in both state estimation and task performance compared to action generation without filtering, using the same pretrained model and no additional test-time information.

Abstract

Safe trajectory tracking (STT) aims to enable autonomous vehicles to follow a reference trajectory accurately while avoiding collisions. This problem is particularly challenging for Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) in highly dynamic environments. Existing non-learning-based STT methods often use simplified vehicle and interaction models to meet real-time requirements, which may result in suboptimal tracking accuracy. Although Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) can help address this limitation, widely used Lagrangian DRL methods often suffer from unstable training because increasing Lagrange multipliers amplify policy-gradient magnitudes. To address these issues, we propose a novel reinforcement learning framework for STT, called STT Control Epigraph Form RL (STTC-EFRL). Experimental results, including Vehicle-in-the-Loop tests, show that STTC-EFRL achieves higher tracking accuracy than the considered non-learning-based baseline and more stable training than widely used Lagrangian DRL methods, while satisfying safety requirements in connected environments.

Abstract

Agents that perform state estimation using pretrained perception models, e.g., Vision-Language Models (VLMs), face the fundamental challenge that predictions are uncertain. Still, most approaches determinize these predictions into a single best-guess state and make decisions as if it were ground truth. This is brittle because when the guess is wrong, the agent's decisions may be unsafe or infeasible. At the other extreme, maintaining a full probability distribution over symbolic states is often intractable. We propose a middle path, drawing on the underlying probabilities the perception model already produces (e.g., a VLM's output-layer token distributions) to construct a $θ$-confidence Minimal Belief Set (MBS$_θ$), a minimal subset of states whose cumulative probability meets a user-specified belief-mass threshold. The minimality of an MBS$_θ$ ensures that the agent considers only the most likely states, enhancing robustness in downstream decision-making while maintaining computational tractability. Using a novel three-layered belief system, we efficiently approximate individual state probabilities from per-feature predictions and task-level constraints. On this belief construction, we base our MBS$_θ$ construction, while maintaining only a lean factored representation of the belief. Our algorithm, Probability-Ranked Valid State Search (PRaVaSS), takes advantage of the unique structure of this belief system to construct an MBS$_θ$ with correctness guarantees and polynomial complexity in the unconstrained case. Using a VLM as the backbone of our state estimator, we validate our method's theoretical properties, scalability with problem size, and improvement of downstream decision-making when used in a task-planning pipeline.

Abstract

Multi-sensor state estimation requires accurate spatial and temporal calibration between heterogeneous sensors. Existing spatiotemporal calibration methods typically represent sensor extrinsic parameters as a rigid-body transformation together with a scalar temporal offset, causing the spatial and temporal parameters to enter the estimation problem through separate mechanisms. While the spatial transformation composes directly with the robot pose, accounting for the temporal offset requires evaluating the trajectory at a shifted time, typically through motion compensation, interpolation, or a continuous-time trajectory representation. Simulated and real experiments demonstrate calibration and trajectory-estimation performance comparable to established methods across heterogeneous sensor configurations. Our simplified framework therefore offers a quality-of-life improvement over existing methods without compromising performance.

Abstract

In this letter, we consider trajectory tracking control and inertial parameter estimation of a quadrotor UAV whose center of mass (CoM) does not coincide with its geometric center. Onboard payloads like an IMU, a camera, or a battery are not necessarily placed at its geometric center. Moreover, during the flight these sensors might move because of external disturbances and cause a CoM offset. Thus, these events and equipment introduce an offset and cause the inertia matrix to be non-diagonal. We develop an integral concurrent learning (ICL)-based geometric controller that estimates the mass, the CoM offset, and the full inertia matrix online, without an accelerometer or numerical derivatives, and tracks a given trajectory corrected by the offset estimated online. We prove that the estimated parameters converge to their true values under a finite excitation condition. We validate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm using a simulation example.
