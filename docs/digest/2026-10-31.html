Abstract

Humanoids have become increasingly agile and capable in real-world environments. In this work, we present Humanoid Dodgeball, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for agile dynamic obstacle avoidance in humanoids. Abstract

Reliable obstacle avoidance is essential for safe quadrotor flight in unknown environments, yet conventional frame-based cameras remain challenged by motion blur, limited dynamic range, and latency under fast motion. Event cameras, with sub-millisecond latency, high dynamic range, and negligible motion blur, offer a promising alternative, but leveraging them for closed-loop control remains difficult. Existing approaches either adopt cascaded architectures that first estimate depth and then map it to control commands, introducing error propagation, or achieve end-to-end control through costly offline pretraining before online fine-tuning can even begin. We address these challenges with a CUDA-based event simulator that exploits GPU parallelism to render events efficiently within the training loop, integrated into a differentiable simulation pipeline that enables direct gradient backpropagation through the differentiable physics model, improving sample efficiency. This allows us to train an end-to-end policy that maps event frames directly to control commands, without intermediate depth estimation. We validate our approach through zero-shot real-world deployment on a quadrotor, achieving robust obstacle avoidance in both odometry-assisted and odometry-free settings, including under low-light conditions where conventional cameras typically fail.

Abstract

In autonomous driving, robot teleoperation serves as a critical safety fallback mechanism, requiring operators to assume direct control in the event of system failure. While recent research has pivoted toward high-level supervisory control or simulation-based studies, these approaches often fail to capture the physical dynamics and sensory demands of driving a full-scale vehicle. To address this gap, we evaluated the impact of spatial data representation on operator performance using a real car-sized vehicle in a field experiment with $N = 18$ participants. We compared three visualization interfaces: (i) a standard camera feed baseline, with two bird's-eye views (BEV) from (ii) a raw LiDAR point cloud, and (iii) a sensor fusion of LiDAR and camera data streams. By synthesizing objective performance data with comprehensive subjective assessments, we demonstrate that BEV fusion maximizes safety margins while minimizing the operator's mental demand. These findings challenge existing teleoperation conventions and provide an empirically validated approach for designing robust remote piloting systems.

Spacecraft-mounted robotic manipulators can support long-term operability of orbiting assets by conducting tasks such as on-orbit assembly, repair, upgrade, and refueling. In this context, the integration of continuum/soft robotic mechanisms into space manipulator designs promises to reduce stowage volume and mass, as well as to improve operational safety through the inherent mechanical compliance of soft structures. Abstract

Filtering has not yet had its foundation-model moment: state estimation is still dominated either by online Bayesian filtering algorithms that require known dy- namics as inputs, or by learning-based neural filters trained from scratch for a single system or parametric family. The core challenge is structural heterogene- ity: dynamical systems differ in their governing dynamics, physical scales, and noise levels, precluding a shared representation space for a reusable filter. To bridge this gap, we introduce FilterFM, the first pretrained model for Bayesian filtering that estimates latent states across heterogeneous dynamical systems with a single checkpoint. Furthermore, we uncover clear scaling behaviors in foundation-model-based Bayesian filtering, which combined with comprehensive component ablation studies, validate the structural optimality of our design and broaden the generalization scope for this research field.

Abstract

Quadrotor trajectory tracking under unmodeled disturbances often relies on online residual adaptation, but short out-domain residuals can corrupt the learned disturbance model when constant-step updates treat every residual gradient uniformly. We propose Dual Gradient Consensus (DGC), an adaptive-learning method for mixed in-domain/out-domain disturbances that retains the random Fourier feature model, offline BPTT policy, and least-squares adaptation of a prior domain-adaptive residual policy (DAPL) while modifying the online update step size. DGC compares each residual gradient with long- and short-term gradient memories, classifies the current adaptation regime, and uses a confidence map to suppress unreliable updates while recovering adaptation after persistent domain shifts. We evaluate DGC in numerical quadrotor trajectory tracking under four residuals that mix an in-domain component with short out-domain events. Compared with $L_1$-augmented MPC, RFF-based MPC, constant-step DAPL, and a frozen-parameter baseline, DGC achieves the lowest mean position RMSE in all four cases.
